Interventional Treatment of Cardiovascular Disease

General information about the procedure. The final indication and preparation are determined by the treating medical team based on each case.

What is it?

Interventional treatment groups together different therapeutic procedures performed by catheterization — such as coronary angioplasty and stent placement — to treat cardiovascular disease without the need for open-heart surgery.

What is it used for?

It is used to treat blockages or other cardiovascular conditions that, based on medical evaluation, can be resolved through a hemodynamics procedure instead of conventional surgery.

How is it performed, in general terms?

The specific approach depends on each patient's diagnosis. In general, access is gained through an arterial puncture under local anesthesia, and specialized catheters and devices (balloons, stents, among others) are used to treat the affected area.

Typical preparation

Specific preparation depends on the procedure indicated and is determined by the treating team beforehand. You can review the general guidelines for preparing for a study, which always work alongside your physician's specific instructions.

What happens afterward?

It depends on the procedure performed. A period of observation and specific recommendations about rest, medication and follow-up checks are usually indicated.
