Coronary Angioplasty

General information about the procedure. The final indication and preparation are determined by the treating medical team based on each case.

What is it?

Coronary angioplasty is an interventional cardiology procedure used to treat narrowing or blockage of a coronary artery, with the goal of restoring blood flow to the heart muscle.

What is it used for?

It is indicated for patients diagnosed with coronary disease, when one or more heart arteries show a significant blockage that limits blood flow. The decision to perform it always depends on an individual medical evaluation, usually after a diagnostic coronary angiography.

How is it performed, in general terms?

Access to the artery is gained through a puncture, generally in the wrist or the groin, under local anesthesia. A catheter is guided to the blocked area, and a small balloon is used to widen the artery; in many cases a stent is also placed to keep it open. The procedure is performed in a room specially equipped for this type of study.

Typical preparation

Specific preparation is indicated by the treating team before the procedure (fasting, regular medication, prior studies, documentation to bring). You can review the general guidelines for preparing for a study, which always work alongside your physician's specific instructions.

What happens afterward?

A period of observation and brief hospitalization for follow-up is usually indicated. The medical team provides specific recommendations about rest, medication and follow-up checks, tailored to each patient.
